## The workaround

A patient searches for a doctor and lands on a big listing site, where the clinic is one card among a dozen. If they look for the clinic's own website, they often find an old page with out-of-date hours, a phone number and no way to pick a time. So they go back to the listing and book there, and the clinic pays a commission on a patient who already chose it. After a good visit, most happy patients go home and never post a review.

## What DocScale does today

**A website of the clinic's own.** Each clinic gets a site on its own web address, with its services, its story, what patients say, and how to reach and find it. It works well on a phone. Staff change it in a simple editor, with themes, colour palettes and starting presets for different specialties. The clinic's blog runs from the same portal.

**Booking that goes straight to the clinic.** A patient taps to book, fills in their details, says what kind of visit it is, and picks a time. The request lands in the clinic's portal, not on someone else's site. The front desk confirms, reschedules or cancels it from the appointment screen.

**A patient list that keeps itself.** Each booking adds the patient to the clinic's list, with how to reach them, when they last came in and how often they've visited.

**Reviews from the visits that went well.** The clinic gets a feedback link and a QR code to print for the front desk. A patient who gives five stars is sent on to leave a public review. A patient who gives fewer can write to the clinic privately instead, and that message lands in the clinic's feedback inbox, so staff hear about a problem first.

**Set up for the clinic.** Our team builds the clinic's portal and sends the invites. Before a clinic signs up, we can build a preview of its site from its name, city and specialty. A simple calculator shows what missed bookings could be costing it each month.

## What's next

Next come the pieces that cut phone time further: an email to the patient each time a booking is confirmed, moved or cancelled, and subscription management from inside the portal. We're also working on helping each blog post reach patients who ask AI assistants for a doctor, not just those who search.

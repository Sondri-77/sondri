## The workaround

A traveler plans a day trip. They search, or more and more often they ask an AI assistant, and the answer points them to a booking platform where the operator is one listing among dozens. If they look for the operator's own website, it rarely ranks, and AI assistants rarely mention it. So they book on the platform, which typically keeps 15 to 30 percent of the sale and the traveler's details with it. The operator ran the trip but doesn't own the customer, the data or the next booking.

## How it works

**Pages for the trips people search for.** We build a page for each destination, route and kind of trip travelers look for, in the languages they search in. Each one answers the real questions: how to get there, what's included, when to go, what to bring.

**Content AI assistants can use.** Answer engines quote sources that state things clearly and back them up. We write and structure the operator's content so ChatGPT, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews can find it, trust it and name the operator in their answers.

**The technical groundwork.** Structured data that tells search engines what each page is about, a site that loads fast on a phone, and a complete Google Business profile with up-to-date hours, photos and reviews.

**A site that takes the booking.** Visibility only pays off if the traveler can book on the spot. The operator's site shows real availability and takes the booking directly, and the traveler's email or WhatsApp number stays with the operator, not a platform.

**Numbers you can check.** Every booking is tracked back to where it came from: search, an AI answer, social or a platform. The operator can see what's working and what each channel is worth.

## What we fix

Every booking that moves from a platform to the operator's own site keeps the commission in the business, and the traveler with it. The operator gets a customer list it can reach again, reviews on a profile it controls, and a website that keeps bringing in bookings long after the work is done.

A traveler used to plan a day trip by typing a few words into Google and clicking through the results. Now many of them ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Overviews instead: "What's the best day trip from the city?" "Is the mountain route worth it in winter?" "Who runs a good small-group tour there?"

The answer they get is a short paragraph, not ten blue links. A few names make it into that paragraph. Everyone else is invisible.

We recently sat down with a tour operator who runs exactly the kind of trip those questions are about. The experience is excellent, the reviews are strong, and most of their bookings still come through third-party platforms. This post is about why that happens and what fixes it.

## Renting your own demand

Booking platforms are good at one thing: being the first place a traveler lands. They rank for every destination search, and they're the sources AI assistants quote most often. So when a traveler goes looking, the platform is what they find.

Zero-click search has accelerated that shift. According to [SparkToro and Datos's 2024 clickstream study](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study-for-every-1000-us-google-searches-only-374-clicks-go-to-the-open-web-in-the-eu-its-360/), **58.5% of US Google searches** end without a click to the open web. And when Google shows an AI Overview, [Ahrefs' February 2026 study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/) found that the top-ranking page sees a **58% lower average click-through rate**.

The cost of relying on third-party platforms is easy to miss because it's spread across every sale. Online travel agencies (OTAs) typically keep 15 to 30 percent of each booking. They also keep the traveler. The operator gets a name on a manifest, but the email address, the review, the chance to sell the next trip and the data on who's booking all stay with the platform.

An operator in that position is renting its own demand. The more popular the destination gets, the more it pays.

## What changed: answers instead of links

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the work of getting a website to rank in search results. It still matters. Google is still where a lot of trip planning starts, and good SEO is the foundation for everything below.

Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the newer half. It's the work of getting a business named and cited when someone asks an AI assistant a question. The rules overlap with SEO but aren't the same:

- **An answer engine picks a handful of sources, not a page of results.** Being the eighth-best result on Google still gets some clicks. Not making the AI's shortlist gets none.
- **It favors content that answers the question directly.** A page that says clearly what the trip is, where it goes, what it costs, how long it takes and who it's for is easier to quote than a page of marketing copy.
- **It looks for agreement across sources.** If the operator's site, its Google Business profile and its reviews all tell the same story, the assistant is more confident naming it.
- **It reads structure.** Structured data, clear headings and question-and-answer content make it easier for any engine to understand what a page is about.

For travel, this matters more than most industries. Trip planning is full of exactly the kind of open questions people now ask an assistant, and the answer usually ends with a name and a link to book.

## Third-party OTA vs. direct website with AEO

| Factor | Third-party booking platform (OTA) | Direct operator site with SEO & AEO |
|---|---|---|
| **Commission per booking** | Typically 15%–30% per sale | 0% platform commission |
| **Customer relationship** | Platform owns email, chat, and repeat marketing | Operator owns email, WhatsApp, and repeat bookings |
| **AI citation target** | Assistant cites the OTA marketplace page | Assistant cites and links the operator directly |
| **Reviews & authority** | Reviews strengthen the platform's domain | Reviews compound on the operator's own site and Google profile |
| **Long-term cost** | Same percentage cut on every future trip | Upfront build; marginal cost drops as content compounds |

## What it takes to show up

None of this is a trick. It's the groundwork most operators never had time to lay.

1. **A page for every trip people look for.** Each destination, route and season gets its own page, written around the questions travelers actually ask, in the languages they ask them in.
2. **Content written to be quoted.** Clear facts up front, specifics over adjectives, and answers to the questions that come up before every booking: how to get there, what to bring, what happens if the weather turns.
3. **The technical basics done properly.** Structured data (`TouristTrip`, `FAQPage`, `LocalBusiness` / `Organization`), a fast site on a phone, and a complete Google Business profile with current hours, photos and reviews.
4. **A site that takes the booking.** Getting found is only half of it. If the traveler lands on the operator's site and can't book, they go back to the platform. Real availability and direct booking turn visibility into revenue.
5. **Measurement from the first booking.** Every booking tracked back to where it came from, so the operator can see whether search, AI answers or a platform is doing the work, and what each one costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) for tour operators?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring an operator's trip pages, pricing, logistics, and structured data so AI assistants—such as ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Gemini, and Perplexity—name the operator and link to its direct booking page instead of routing travelers to a third-party marketplace.

### Does AEO replace traditional SEO?

No. Google's AI Overviews and assistant search tools still draw heavily from indexed, authoritative search results. Technical SEO (crawlability, fast mobile loading, clean headings, and schema markup) is the base layer; AEO adds direct-answer formatting, consistent third-party entity profiles, and machine-readable clarity on top.

## Why it compounds

Platform bookings cost the same every time. Owned visibility works the other way. A destination page written this year keeps bringing in travelers next year. Each direct booking adds a customer the operator can reach again, and a review on a profile it controls. Each review and each well-answered question makes the next AI answer a little more likely to name the operator.

Popular destinations make this urgent. When demand rises, the platforms take their cut of all of it. The operators who own their visibility keep it.
